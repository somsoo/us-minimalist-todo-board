          📖 Executive Prioritization: The Eisenhower Principle

          Attributed to 34th US President Dwight D. Eisenhower and popularized by Dr. Stephen Covey in The 7 Habits of Highly Effective People, this model distinguishes between urgency and significance:

          • The Urgent/Important Distinction: "What is important is seldom urgent, and what is urgent is seldom important." Urgent tasks demand immediate reaction; important tasks contribute directly to long-term goals and strategic values.
          • Quadrant 2 Dominance: High performers allocate disproportionate time to Quadrant 2 (Not Urgent, but Important)—proactive planning, relationship building, skill development, and health preservation.
          • Minimizing Quadrants 3 and 4: Urgent non-important tasks (Q3) should be automated or delegated, while time-wasting non-important non-urgent activities (Q4) should be ruthlessly eliminated.

          Productivity references: Covey S. (1989) The 7 Habits of Highly Effective People; Eisenhower D. (1954) Address to the Second Assembly of the World Council of Churches.

          ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

          What is the Eisenhower Decision Matrix?

          The Eisenhower Matrix divides tasks into four quadrants based on Urgency and Importance: Q1 (Do First), Q2 (Schedule/Plan), Q3 (Delegate), and Q4 (Eliminate).

          Why is Quadrant 2 considered the most critical?

          Quadrant 2 contains high-impact strategic tasks that are Important but Not Urgent (such as career planning, exercise, and relationship building). Focusing on Q2 prevents continuous crisis management.

          Can I move tasks between different quadrants?

          Yes. Each task features a quadrant selector dropdown allowing you to reclassify tasks instantly as priorities shift.

          Are my tasks or confidential to-do items stored online?

          No. All tasks, matrix states, and completion histories are stored exclusively in your browser local storage. No task data is transmitted to external servers.

          ⚠️ Note on Task Overload

          Limit Quadrant 1 tasks to fewer than 5 critical action items per day. Having more than 5 urgent and important tasks indicates chronic planning deficits rather than true operational emergencies.
